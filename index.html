Mahindra & Mahindra: the growth is real; the price is finally more reasonable

M&M has turned a broad portfolio into fast growth: FY26 revenue rose 25%, PAT rose 32%, and Auto, Farm and Services each contributed to the group story. I would Accumulate the stock for a 12-month view, with an illustrative fair-value range of ₹3,800–₹4,200—provided investors accept that auto margins, farm demand and finance-book quality can all turn quickly.

The case is no longer “buy a great company at any price.” At 21.1x trailing earnings, M&M trades below its reported five-year average and below the two passenger-vehicle/two-wheeler peers used here for context. That discount is deserved in part: M&M is a diversified group with financial-services exposure, not a pure-play automaker.

A business with three earnings engines

M&M’s core is Auto and Farm, but investors buy a group. FY26 segment revenue before eliminations was ₹1,17,834 crore in Auto, ₹42,568 crore in Farm and ₹43,698 crore in Services. The segments cannot be added to reproduce group revenue because eliminations and reporting scope differ.

Auto is the growth engine. M&M’s company-reported SUV revenue share reached 25.3% in FY26, up 260 basis points year on year; the company also reported 52.3% share in LCVs below 3.5 tonnes. Farm is the steadier franchise: tractor share was 43.6%, a 30-basis-point gain, and the company said it retained the market lead for a 43rd consecutive year. M&M’s FY26 results and integrated report document those positions.

The moat is distribution, product breadth and repeat customer economics, rather than an unassailable technology lead. A tractor customer may return for service, implements and replacement; a strong SUV franchise gives dealers and suppliers volume to build around. The company’s electric SUV launches—the XEV 9e and BE 6—extend the product range, but they do not yet prove that EVs will earn mature Auto margins. The FY26 integrated report records the launches and their recognition.

Services is not one business. It combines Financial Services with technology, logistics and other industrial and consumer services. That diversity can steady earnings, but it also makes group borrowings and cash flow harder to read as if M&M were only a vehicle manufacturer.

FY21–FY26: scale up, margins improve, cash still needs watching

Income from operations increased from ₹74,278 crore in FY21 to ₹1,98,639 crore in FY26, a roughly 21.7% five-year CAGR. FY26 growth was 25%. PAT after non-controlling interests rose from ₹3,347 crore to ₹17,099 crore, up 32% in FY26. The FY21 PAT figure is on a continuing-operations basis and is not strictly comparable with later years.

PAT margin moved from 4.5% in FY21 to 8.6% in FY26. It reached 8.5% in FY23, eased to 8.1% in FY24–FY25, then recovered. That shape matters: the profit improvement is substantial, but not a straight-line march. FY26 return on equity was 20.1%, a strong result that investors should expect management to defend through the cycle.

The segment mix shows where quality comes from. In FY26, Auto generated ₹10,383 crore of PBIT on ₹1,17,834 crore revenue, an 8.8% margin, versus 8.6% in FY25. Farm generated ₹5,517 crore of PBIT on ₹42,568 crore revenue, a 13.0% margin, down from 14.0%. Services recorded ₹7,483 crore of PBIT and a 17.1% margin, up from 15.3%; the company defines this bucket across Financial Services and industrial and consumer services. These are different businesses, so the margin comparison explains mix—it is not a peer ranking. The company’s FY26 results release provides the segment numbers.

Operating cash flow improved to ₹11,657 crore from ₹3,176 crore in FY25, about 68% of FY26 PAT. But investing outflow was ₹17,707 crore and financing inflow ₹5,391 crore. Financial Services lending and collections can make operating cash flow swing; gross borrowings of ₹1,29,615 crore include finance-company funding. I would track finance-book asset quality, segment earnings and cash generation together, rather than label all group debt “automotive leverage.”

Q2 FY27: growth is broad, but the quality is uneven

For the quarter ended 30 September 2026, consolidated revenue was ₹57,553 crore and PAT after NCI was ₹4,588 crore, each up 25% year on year. H1 FY27 revenue was about 26% above H1 FY26. Auto consolidated PBIT margin reached 8.8%, 110 basis points higher sequentially. M&M’s Q2 FY27 release attributes the quarter’s group PAT growth to execution across businesses and notes that Farm saw a festive-season shift and weaker quarterly performance.

My read: this is a strong growth print, not a blanket all-clear. Auto is carrying momentum; Farm remains exposed to seasonality and input costs; Services’ growth needs the finance-book risk lens. The next evidence to watch is whether Auto margins hold while commodity costs rise, and whether Farm recovers without a price-led market-share trade-off.

Valuation: a discount, not a bargain-bin multiple

At the supplied snapshot, M&M’s 21.1x trailing P/E is below the 25.5x five-year average reported by Trendlyne. That is a meaningful reset in expectations. It is not proof that the shares are cheap: the multiple can stay below its average if growth slows or finance-related earnings become less dependable.

For context, Screener’s Maruti page showed about 30.7x and its Bajaj Auto page about 26.0x when checked for this article. These are platform-reported P/Es, not perfectly synchronized calculations. Maruti’s company-only auto exposure and Bajaj’s two-/three-wheeler mix differ from M&M’s consolidated group. Treat the gap as context, not evidence that one is automatically cheaper. Tata CV is a successor entity and Tata PV includes JLR and a reported loss; their P/Es are not clean comparables here.

Catalysts and four risks that can change the call

Catalysts: Continued SUV mix and market-share gains can support Auto volumes and margins. A hold in Auto’s 8.8% consolidated PBIT margin would show that growth is not being bought with discounts. EV volumes from the XEV 9e and BE 6 can broaden the franchise if the economics follow the launches. Farm’s 43.6% share and Services’ 17.1% FY26 segment PBIT margin provide additional earnings levers, while Q2’s 25% group PAT growth gives the market a higher near-term base to judge.

Risks:

  1. SUV competition and commodity inflation. Rival launches or steel, aluminium and other input-cost spikes can force discounting or compress Auto’s 8.8% PBIT margin. The FY26 report discusses commodity and geopolitical cost uncertainty; Q2 management also cited significant commodity hikes. Annual report risk discussion and Q2 FY27 results.
  2. Farm-cycle and weather risk. Weak rainfall, farm income or financing affordability can delay tractor purchases. A prolonged slowdown would hit Farm revenue and could pull its FY26 13.0% PBIT margin lower. M&M’s FY26 risk discussion explicitly identifies monsoon, farmer profitability and machinery demand as sensitivities.
  3. Financial Services credit quality. Rising delinquencies or credit costs would require provisions, cut subsidiary profits and weaken the group cash narrative. FY26 gross borrowings include this funding structure; that is why investors should monitor the finance portfolio rather than infer industrial debt from consolidated borrowings alone. M&M’s FY26 report describes its finance business and asset-quality measures.
  4. EV execution and capital payback. If new electric models fail to sustain demand or pricing, fixed costs, investment and discounting can dilute Auto returns even while reported volumes grow. The XEV 9e and BE 6 are catalysts, but adoption and profitability still have to be earned; the company’s FY26 report establishes the launch context, not guaranteed returns.

Verdict: Accumulate, with a margin-of-safety mindset

M&M has the growth, market positions and improving Auto profitability to merit an Accumulate view. I would not call it a low-risk compounder: Farm is cyclical, the services bucket is diverse, and EV investment raises the execution bar. At 21.1x trailing earnings and below the reported five-year average, the valuation is more acceptable than the operating momentum alone might suggest.

The view is wrong if revenue keeps growing but segment PBIT margins deteriorate, if finance-book stress turns into a persistent earnings drag, or if EV growth requires uneconomic pricing. Watch quarterly Auto and Farm margins, finance asset quality, cash flow against PAT, and the market price relative to the base-case range. Add gradually; do not underwrite the bull case before the numbers do.
